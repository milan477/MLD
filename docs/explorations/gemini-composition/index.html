Can state-of-the-art audio language models dissect my composition?

I asked five audio language models to “identify all audible sound sources in each section, including background and overlapping sources” in a piece I composed. The best score was 5.5 out of 16.

None of them heard the piano waltz I composed in F minor/major. All that work, only for audio language models to completely not hear it!

They also missed the frogs, the donkey Balthazar, the rocket engine, the playing cards being shuffled, and the magical musical flourish. All, except Gemini 4 Argon, also missed the accordion.

Balthazar was variously described as human screaming, a siren, or other animals. He too, he told me, felt let down by the technology. Imagine he had been calling 911 and an audio-language model picked up the phone!

The results made me wonder whether my composition style was maybe out of distribution for the models. I therefore decided to ask a simpler question: "What time signature is my improvisation in?"

I conclude that no model was able to feel the beat of my music: Meta Muse Wonder was close: 7/8 indeed, but grouped 3+2+2, not 2+2+3. The other models were vibing on different music (4/4, 3/4) I believe...

Qwen3.8 Omni Flash

The piece is in 3/4 time – a triple meter. Throughout the work the figuration (the opening motor rhythm, the chordal "ta‑ta‑TA" patterns, the octave ostinato, and the final fast section) consistently groups in threes, giving the piece its driving 3/4 pulse, even in the slower, more rubato middle passage.

Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro

Based on the rhythmic structure and the grouping of beats heard throughout the piece, the time signature is 4/4 (common time). This can be heard clearly in both the fast, driving sections (where the beats group into four distinct pulses per measure) and the slower, lyrical middle section.
